02 / Physics → ShotMap

Simulate the physics.
Distill the intelligence.

Behind every moving shot is our physics-based ShotMap. We do the expensive trajectory search ahead of time, fit the results into compact polynomials, and evaluate those equations on the robot.

Our runtime launch model
f(d, vᵣ) = c₀ + c₁d + c₂vᵣ + c₃d² + c₄dvᵣ + c₅vᵣ²

Two fitted surfaces. Six terms each. Coordinated hood and flywheel commands.

01 · Model

Define the physics

Gravity, launch height, Fuel radius, Hub geometry, hood calibration, and flywheel-to-exit-speed mapping establish the search space.

02 · Search

Sweep the launch space

Sample distance and radial velocity. At each state, sweep hood position and exit speed, then evaluate the resulting ballistic flight.

03 · Select

Choose useful trajectories

Opening intersection, lip clearance, and descending entry angle establish feasibility. Margin, speed headroom, flight time, and hood travel shape candidate ranking.

04 · Compress

Fit and generate

Least-squares fitting produces two quadratic surfaces. One generator writes matching Java and TypeScript coefficients and records maximum fit residuals.

From simulated sample to robot command +

The sampling grid: 1.5–8.0 m distance and −2.5–2.5 m/s radial velocity, both at 0.25 increments. Candidate search steps through hood position at 0.025 motor rotations and exit speed at 0.05 m/s. Feasible samples enter the fit.

The numerical method: the basis [1, d, vᵣ, d², dvᵣ, vᵣ²] forms a six-by-six normal system. Pivoted elimination solves each output surface. The generator evaluates the largest sample residual for hood and roller independently.

The runtime sequence: predict release, evaluate the launch map, cancel tangential motion, transform the launch vector into the robot frame, and evaluate the final trajectory. Close-range Hub shots and passbacks use bounded direct candidate search.

Inside the trajectory evaluator +

Hub shots: generated polynomial commands map distance and radial velocity to launch settings. A bounded direct search covers close-range shots. The final compensated trajectory determines opening intersection, front-lip clearance, and descending entry angle.

Passback: the software selects a field destination and searches bounded launch candidates for a flat, fast transfer. Both shot modes share the final trajectory evaluator and coordinated actuator commands.

Selection logic: geometric feasibility decides whether a candidate can be used. A separate robustness heuristic ranks candidate margins and sensitivity.

Three axes of the launch, three closed loops +

Turret yaw: field-to-robot transformation, nearest equivalent legal target, profiled position control, and timestamp-aware position feedback. Automatic feeding uses the turret’s configured alignment band.

Hood elevation: launch-angle calibration converts between physical pitch and hood motor rotations. Motion Magic drives the hood toward the coordinated launch command.

Flywheel velocity: a calibrated exit-speed mapping produces signed roller RPS. Motion Magic velocity control shapes spin-up, while separate entry and running tolerance bands preserve feed continuity.

Put the driver in command

Immediate manual feed

A deliberate driver input starts both feed motors immediately using the current bounded actuator commands. Releasing it returns control to automatic sequencing.

The control decisions behind the sequence +

Continuous evaluation: readiness combines solution freshness, flywheel speed, hood position, turret alignment, legal setpoints, and motion. Current and predicted release speed both participate; raw and filtered acceleration are evaluated together.

Automatic recovery: when readiness changes, commanded feed stops. Recovery requires 50 ms of continuous readiness, then the 50 ms final-feed delay and an additional 25 ms spindexer delay.

Driver authority: right trigger requests shooting; adding right-stick click requests immediate force feed. Autonomous uses the automatic readiness sequence.

Holonomic path following

PathPlanner’s holonomic controller closes translation and rotation error using the drivetrain pose and chassis speeds. A dashboard chooser selects composed autonomous routines.

Alliance-aware execution

AutoBuilder uses alliance information for mirroring. Named commands expose intake, shot sequence, auto-aim enable, and sequence end actions to the route author.

Destination-based autopilot

At command start, a goal within 0.75 m selects direct alignment; farther goals select pathfinding. Alignment uses X/Y PID and a profiled continuous-input heading controller.

Deliberate completion

The short-range alignment controller requires position and heading tolerances together for a settling interval. Command completion stops the requested chassis motion.

09 / The rest of the robot

Sophistication reaches
every mechanism.

The launch system is one part of a command-based robot. Driver input, swerve, intake deployment, Fuel transport, and lifecycle management all contribute to the same coordinated machine.

01 / Swerve

Motion with intent

Field-centric teleop translates driver input into field motion. Alliance perspective, brake requests, shooting speed caps, vector acceleration ramps, and measured-motion estimation connect driving to shot planning.

Engineering underneath +

Phoenix swerve state feeds the shared motion estimator. Separate SysId routines characterize translation, steering, and rotation. The desktop drivetrain simulation updates on a configured 4 ms loop.

02 / Intake

Controlled deployment

A leader/follower extension mechanism uses profiled positioning while an independently controlled roller collects Fuel. The extension and roller have explicit configuration and enabled states.

Homing and active recovery +

A hard-stop homing state machine distinguishes running, successful, timed-out, and interrupted outcomes. Anti-jam pulse commands alternate extension targets with stall-aware waits, per-step timeouts, and dwell intervals.

03 / Fuel transport

Two stages, one stream

The spindexer supplies the final feed, and both use commanded velocity control. The feed sequencer starts the downstream stage first, adds upstream transport, and continuously reevaluates shot readiness.

Explicit transitions +

Readiness, recovery, staging, continuous feed, and manual override are represented as timestamped state transitions. The same transitions enter the synchronized recorder for later inspection.

04 / Command architecture

Clear ownership

WPILib subsystem requirements define who controls each mechanism. Default drive and auto-aim commands run continuously; composed commands coordinate higher-level actions.

Lifecycle and configuration +

Autonomous commands are scheduled on autonomous entry and canceled for teleop. Test mode clears the scheduler. Named device configuration records retain application status and retry count, while timestamp and joystick replay preserve control inputs.

Event-centered analysis

The analyzer extracts minimum voltage, voltage-dip intervals, recorded brownout events, shooter recovery intervals, and intake movement windows. Event windows bring neighboring samples into view.

Per-mechanism demand

Supply-current streams identify motor demand on the battery. PDH aggregate current and summed motor supply current remain distinct, while stator current describes torque-producing motor current.

Time-aware data handling

The parser accepts direct recorder names, sorts timestamps, and preserves missing samples. Timestamp gaps keep separate events separate, and duplicate timestamps are reported explicitly.

The recorder behind the analysis +

Shared context: mode, match time, shooting request, target mode, solver reason, feed blockers, manual override, and feed transition timestamps sit beside pose motion and actuator targets.

Electrical and device health: PDH voltage/current, roboRIO supply state, motor supply/stator currents, voltage, temperature, faults, resets, signal age, and CAN health describe the same operating interval.

Sampling architecture: configured motor electrical signals request 50 Hz, dedicated turret motion sampling runs at 100 Hz, slow health records at 10 Hz, and the power dashboard refreshes at 5 Hz. Battery and configuration labels identify the run.

12 / Go deeper

Ask us about the decisions.

The interesting engineering is in how the pieces work together.

What makes your moving-shot approach distinctive?+

We plan around the predicted release state. The solver combines translation, acceleration, rotation, turret-pivot offset, and robot orientation before commanding the launch. The feed controller uses the same motion estimate, connecting the physics of the shot to the exact moment we move Fuel into the shooter.

How do you balance motion freedom and shooting?+

We coordinate commanded motion with measured motion. Shooting applies configurable drivetrain speed limits and smooth acceleration ramps. The feed decision checks both current and predicted release motion, while flywheel hysteresis keeps normal speed variation from repeatedly restarting the sequence.

How does the system handle changing camera visibility?+

Vision is fused into an odometry-based pose estimate. We preserve capture timestamps and track the age of accepted observations. The drivetrain continues propagating position between observations, and shot readiness remains a distinct decision based on the trajectory, mechanisms, and motion state.

How do you explain a shot after it happens?+

We record the selected shot model, target mode, motion state, actuator targets, feed decision, and transition timing alongside electrical and system health. That shared timeline gives us a direct path from a robot action back to the software decision that produced it.
